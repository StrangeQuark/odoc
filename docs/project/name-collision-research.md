# Odoc collision and replacement-name research

**Status:** preliminary factual screen; not legal clearance

**Roadmap package:** `P0-001`

**Research date:** 2026-08-14

## Recommendation

Do not adopt **Odoc** as the public product, package, CLI, domain, or project name.
Select and screen a replacement before `P0-002` fixes Java packages, artifact IDs,
runtime names, image/chart names, or frontend branding.

The decisive product-confusion issue is an exact-name, actively maintained developer
documentation generator. Additional package, commercial-software, public trademark,
and domain records make the collision risk materially worse. This screen does not
determine legal rights and is not a substitute for qualified name/trademark review.

## Direct documentation-tool collision

The OCaml Platform already uses [`odoc`](https://ocaml.org/p/odoc/latest) for an active
documentation generator. The official package page describes it as a documentation
tool, lists version 3.2.1 published on 2026-05-12, and reports 1,813 dependent packages
at the research date. It has active
[official documentation](https://ocaml.github.io/odoc/odoc/index.html), an
[official GitHub repository](https://github.com/ocaml/odoc), and documented
[Dune integration](https://dune.readthedocs.io/en/latest/documentation.html).

For another developer-documentation project, this creates immediate search, support,
package/CLI, issue-reporting, source-reference, and community confusion even without
reaching a legal conclusion.

The initial exact-name screen was not sufficient to approve a finalist. A broader
read-only screen on 2026-08-14 checked confusingly similar marks, adjacent products,
active namespaces, and ordinary search results. It found material product-confusion or
launch-operability issues for every original finalist:

| Candidate | Decision | Material evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Tetherleaf** | Reject | The live US standard-character mark [TECHERLEAF, serial 97144677 / registration 6936111](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn97144677), is a one-letter-near mark for consumer electronics and computer peripherals. Tether Operations also has current TETHER software/searchable-database and SaaS applications ([serial 98661006](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn98661006) and [serial 98661020](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn98661020)), while unrelated developer products already use the term, including [larsderidder/tether](https://github.com/larsderidder/tether) and [Tether](https://usetether.dev/). The combined spelling, mark, and market risks outweigh the clean exact-name package/domain result. |
| **Quirelia** | Reject | QUIRE is directly occupied in the target field by [Getty's registered publishing-software mark](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn97056903) and [open-source Quire tool](https://quire.getty.edu/), [Quire LLC's registered technical-report SaaS mark](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn97126709) and [OpenQuire product](https://www.openquire.com/), and [Potix's registered document-collaboration mark](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn86178104) and [Quire Document](https://quire.io/blog/p/document.html). The obvious product shorthand would therefore be a same-field name already in use. |
| **Arborquill** | Reject | The exact [GitHub account](https://github.com/Arborquill) is active and maintains a [JavaScript/Notion-tracking repository](https://github.com/Arborquill/GiantClan), preventing a clean open-source namespace. PointBlue Technology also markets both an Arbor LDAP browser and a Quill native WYSIWYG Markdown editor in the [same software portfolio](https://www.pointbluetech.com/), while [Quill](https://quilljs.com/) is already a major developer-facing rich-text editor. |

These are product/name-screen decisions, not conclusions about legal rights. They are
preserved so a later agent does not repeat a clean exact-match result and silently
restore a rejected candidate.

repository names; npm, PyPI, Maven Central, crates.io, RubyGems, Docker Hub, Quay, and

Earlier generated candidates—Lorespan, Codalume, Vellune, Knowlith, Quillmesh,
Loreframe, Sourceweave, Folivra, and Scriptora—were not advanced after preliminary
domain, repository, software, or category checks. That preliminary result is not a
durable legal rejection; any future reconsideration must repeat and record the full
screen rather than relying on this summary.

An RDAP `404` or registry-WHOIS no-record response is only a point-in-time absence of a
registry object. It does not guarantee registrar availability, price, reservation
status, or legal clearance.
Package and account searches are similarly time-bound. The `.io` lookup used the
relevant registry operator's RDAP endpoint where available and registry WHOIS in other
research runs because `.io` was not present in IANA's DNS RDAP bootstrap at the research
time; the WHOIS service itself did not claim authoritative status. Those results carry
an additional completeness caveat. USPTO searching is only a US knockout and does not
replace review of WIPO, EUIPO, national registers, common-law use, companies, language
meaning, or confusing similarity.

### Reproducibility record

All endpoint requests used HTTPS except the explicitly identified `.io` registry-WHOIS
fallback. Candidate names were normalized to lowercase for package/domain endpoints and
uppercase for exact US word-mark queries. The reusable request shapes were:

```text
api.github.com/users/<candidate>
api.github.com/search/repositories?q=<candidate>+in:name
registry.npmjs.org/<candidate>
registry.npmjs.org/-/v1/search?text=<candidate>
pypi.org/pypi/<candidate>/json
crates.io/api/v1/crates/<candidate>
rubygems.org/api/v1/gems/<candidate>.json
search.maven.org/solrsearch/select?q=<candidate>&rows=20&wt=json
hub.docker.com/v2/search/repositories/?query=<candidate>
quay.io/api/v1/find/repositories?query=<candidate>
artifacthub.io/api/v1/packages/search?ts_query_web=<candidate>&limit=20
rdap.verisign.com/{com|net}/v1/domain/<candidate>.<tld>
rdap.publicinterestregistry.org/rdap/domain/<candidate>.org
pubapi.registry.google/rdap/domain/<candidate>.{dev|app}
rdap.identitydigital.services/rdap/domain/<candidate>.{ai|io}
```

At the recorded research time, Schemalyth and Dovureli each returned GitHub user 404,
GitHub repository-name count 0, npm exact 404/search count 0, PyPI 404, Maven count 0,
crates.io 404, RubyGems 404, Docker Hub count 0, Quay count 0, and Artifact Hub count 0.
The listed domain checks returned RDAP 404/no object, except Dovureli's `.io` check used
the registry operator's port-43 WHOIS service and returned `Domain not found`.
Tetherleaf's expanded `.io` check used the same fallback; Schemalyth's used the listed
Identity Digital RDAP endpoint. A later automated client can receive rate-limit or
anti-bot responses, so a different response must be recorded rather than
silently treated as absence.

USPTO searches used its public `tmsearch` POST endpoint with `track_total_hits: true`.
The exact request matched `WM` using `match_phrase`; the fuzzy request used
`query_string` with `<candidate>~2` against `wordmark`; the prefix check used the same
word-mark field. Exact and prefix totals were 0 for both retained candidates at the
research time. The material fuzzy results are named in the table above. The public
endpoint is undocumented/subject to change, so the request date, result count, mark,
serial, status, class, and goods/services must be captured again during qualified legal
review.

`P0-001` remains pending until the owner selects one candidate, the selected candidate
receives qualified legal review, and the owner separately records whether to adopt
or replace it. Candidate selection is not clearance or public-name adoption. This
research does not authorize a purchase, reservation, repository rename, remote change,
package rename, or public announcement.

## Scope and limitations

This was a read-only screen of official project/package pages, public software
registries, registry RDAP/WHOIS, web results, USPTO search/TSDR, and a government
Gazette. It did not comprehensively search common-law use, companies, social accounts,
every package/container/chart registry, WIPO, EUIPO, national offices, or every
relevant jurisdiction/class. Dates and statuses can change.

Before adopting a replacement:

1. screen exact and confusingly similar names across documentation/knowledge software,
   developer tools, collaboration/SaaS, GitHub, Maven/npm/PyPI/container/Helm ecosystems,
   domains, and relevant trademark/company databases;
2. record spelling, pronunciation, intended classes/jurisdictions, owners, and sources;
3. obtain qualified legal review appropriate to the intended distribution/use;
4. confirm project-owner approval before reserving external names or changing the two
   repository remotes; and
5. keep the old repository names only as temporary locators until a coordinated,
   documented migration is authorized.
